# Ten material choices architects don't know they're making blind

**The single most consequential sustainability decision in a building happens not at the structural level but in finish and envelope material selection — and most of those decisions are made without quantified data.** Across 10 common product categories, the gap between the best and worst available options ranges from 2× to 100× in embodied carbon, with equally dramatic differences in occupant health exposure and lifecycle cost. This report provides the specific, citable numbers behind each gap, framed as quiz-style reveals for a museum exhibit targeting US architects. Every comparison includes named products, EPD/HPD citations, animation concepts, and flagged tradeoffs.

The data comes primarily from product-specific EPDs verified to ISO 14025, peer-reviewed LCAs, and Health Product Declarations. Where data is absent, that absence is itself a finding — and a core exhibit message: *we routinely select materials that touch every occupant's lungs without requiring the same disclosure we demand for a bag of chips.*

## 1. Insulation: the blowing agent that outweighs the building

### The data

Legacy XPS insulation (Owens Corning Foamular, pre-NGX formulation) carries a total lifecycle GWP of **57.8 kg CO₂e per m² at RSI-1** (R-5.68), of which **28.5 kg CO₂e — 49% of the total — comes from blowing agent diffusion during the use phase alone** (stage B1 over 75 years). The blowing agent is HFC-134a, with a global warming potential of **1,430× CO₂** per IPCC AR5. By contrast, ROCKWOOL Comfortbatt mineral wool registers approximately **1.07–1.4 kg CO₂e per m² at RSI-1**, and dense-pack cellulose (GreenFiber) can be **net carbon-negative at −88.6 kg CO₂e per 100 ft² at R-20** when biogenic carbon sequestration is credited.

The ratio: **legacy XPS is 30–100× higher in GWP than mineral wool or cellulose at equivalent R-value.** Even Owens Corning's next-generation FOAMULAR NGX (using HFO Opteon 1100 blowing agent, GWP <80) still runs approximately **2× higher than non-XPS rigid alternatives.**

The spray foam health story adds a second dimension. A 2019 peer-reviewed study by Bello et al. in the *International Journal of Hygiene and Environmental Health* found that **16% of spray foam applicator breathing-zone samples exceeded the NIOSH recommended exposure limit of 50 μg/m³ for MDI** (methylene diphenyl diisocyanate), a potent respiratory sensitizer. The EPA recommends occupants vacate for **24–72 hours** post-installation.

| Insulation Type | GWP (kg CO₂e/m² at RSI-1) | Blowing Agent GWP | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legacy XPS (HFC-134a) | **57.8** | 1,430× CO₂ | Owens Corning Foamular EPD (2020), product-specific |
| NGX XPS (HFO) | ~11.5 | <80× CO₂ | Owens Corning/CLF community post (2024) |
| EPS (pentane) | **1.9–3.5** | ~4–7× CO₂ | Hill et al., *Energy & Buildings* (peer-reviewed meta-analysis) |
| Mineral wool (ROCKWOOL) | **1.07–1.4** | N/A (no blowing agent) | ROCKWOOL EPD IBU-EPD-RWI-20240078 (2025) |
| Dense-pack cellulose | **~0.3–1.0** (potentially negative) | N/A | Efficiency Vermont 2019 GHG Taskforce Report; KPMB Lab 2021 |

**Sources:** Owens Corning Foamular XPS EPD (2020): transparencycatalog.com. KPMB Lab, "Embodied Carbon Values in Common Insulation Materials," May 2021: kpmb.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/KPMB-LAB_Embodied-Carbon-in-Insulation.pdf. Efficiency Vermont GHG Taskforce (2019): efficiencyvermont.com/Media/Default/docs/white-papers/2019_RD_GHG_taskforce.pdf. Bello et al. (2019), *Int. J. Hygiene Environ. Health*, DOI: 10.1016/j.ijheh.2019.04.010. ROCKWOOL EPD (2025): epd.guide/epd-industry-overviews/stone-wool-insulation-gwp-battle-of-three-titans.

### Quiz question

**"You're insulating a roof. How much more global warming impact does traditional XPS rigid foam have compared to mineral wool at the same R-value?"**
- A) About the same (±10%)
- B) 3× more
- C) 10× more
- D) 40× or more ✅

*Reveal: Legacy XPS's blowing agent alone — the gas trapped inside the foam — has a warming potential 1,430 times that of CO₂. Over a building's life, nearly half the climate damage from XPS comes not from manufacturing it, but from the gas slowly leaking out of it.*

### Named products

- **Worst performer:** Owens Corning FOAMULAR (legacy HFC-134a) — product-specific EPD available at Transparency Catalog
- **Transitional:** Owens Corning FOAMULAR NGX — ~80% GWP reduction; EPD on UL SPOT
- **Low-carbon rigid:** Atlas Molded Products EPS — pentane blowing agent (GWP ~5)
- **Lowest GWP:** ROCKWOOL Comfortbatt — EPD available; GreenFiber cellulose — negative carbon with biogenic credit
- **Health concern:** Closed-cell spray foam — no standardized HPD; EPA recommends 24–72 hr vacancy

### Animation concept

A single XPS board slowly exhales a rising plume of animated HFC-134a molecules, each tagged "1,430× CO₂." A mineral wool batt of the same R-value sits inert beside it. A counter tallies cumulative warming: by year 75, the XPS plume has accumulated 28.5 kg CO₂e while the mineral wool counter barely moves. A timeline slider lets visitors scrub through the building's life and watch the gap widen.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **XPS provides superior moisture resistance** for below-grade applications — mineral wool and cellulose are not direct substitutes in all assemblies
- Polyiso (pentane blowing agent, GWP ~4) **loses 20–30% of its R-value below 25°F** — critical cold-climate caveat
- The KPMB Lab study found that in an electric heat pump scenario, **legacy XPS embodied carbon may never be repaid** by operational savings
- Cellulose's negative GWP includes biogenic carbon credit, which is debated methodologically
- Data is cradle-to-gate with options (A1–A3, B1, C4) for XPS; cellulose data uses different boundary

## 2. Interior paint: when "zero-VOC" isn't zero

### The data

A 2015 peer-reviewed study by Schieweck and Bock (Fraunhofer Institute/TU Braunschweig, *Building and Environment*, DOI: 10.1016/j.buildenv.2014.11.031) tested US "green" certified paints and found **"no significant difference in emissions between conventional paints and the low-VOC and zero-VOC paints; the ultra-low VOC paints showed the highest emission potential"** — because manufacturers shifted from regulated VOCs to **unregulated semi-volatile organic compounds (SVOCs)** used as solvents, plasticizers, and additives.

A 2024 study analyzed 40 best-selling water-based paints globally and detected **20 SVOCs at concentrations of 10–35,000 ppm in dry films**, with **isothiazolinone preservatives** (linked to severe allergies and asthma) found in approximately **50% of samples.** In 24 "zero- or low-VOC" wet paint samples, **11 different VOCs were detected** including ethylene glycol at 800–20,000 ppm.

The EPA confirms: *"VOC labels and certification programs may not properly assess all of the VOCs emitted from the product…This is especially true of most wet products, such as paints or adhesives that may be labeled as 'low-VOC' or 'zero VOC.'"*

Only one North American paint manufacturer — **ECOS Paints** — has achieved both an HPD and a Declare label with **Red List Free** designation. Meanwhile, Benjamin Moore Natura (marketed as zero-VOC, Cradle to Cradle Silver) was **discontinued in March 2021** and faced an FTC complaint regarding environmental marketing claims. Its replacement, Eco Spec, contains isothiazolinone biocides. Sherwin-Williams Harmony, marketed as zero-VOC, was found by the National Advertising Division to produce **42–112 g/L VOC when tinted with conventional colorants** in dark shades.

On embodied carbon, a mineral/lime paint (Ambient Pro+) registers **0.089 kg CO₂e per m² per coat** versus **0.492 kg CO₂e per m²** for a conventional acrylic — a **5.5× difference.**

### Quiz question

**"A paint label says 'Zero VOC.' After it dries on your wall, how many semi-volatile organic compounds (SVOCs) might it still be emitting into the room?"**
- A) Zero — that's what the label means
- B) 1–3 trace compounds
- C) Up to 20 different SVOCs at concentrations up to 35,000 ppm ✅
- D) Only if the room is above 90°F

*Reveal: "Zero VOC" measures only what evaporates during a standardized test. Manufacturers increasingly use semi-volatile compounds — heavier molecules that escape the VOC definition but still migrate into indoor air and dust for years. The EPA itself warns that VOC labels "may not properly assess all of the VOCs emitted."*

### Named products

- **Red List Free with HPD + Declare:** ECOS Paints — Declare ID IPA-1002, CDPH 01350 compliant, ASTM D-4236 (non-toxic). ecospaints.net
- **Widely specified, some transparency:** Benjamin Moore Eco Spec — Green Seal 11, zero-VOC, APE-free, but contains isothiazolinone; Ultra Spec has Declare label (confirmed PFAS-free)
- **Marketing vs. reality case study:** Sherwin-Williams Harmony — GREENGUARD Gold certified, but NAD found 42–112 g/L VOC in dark tints with conventional colorants
- **No HPD benchmark:** Behr, Valspar, Glidden — major market-share brands with no HPDs or Declare labels

### Animation concept

A wall section with wet paint dries on-screen. A "VOC" meter drops to zero — the visitor sees the "zero-VOC" label appear. Then a second meter labeled "SVOCs" slowly rises, showing 20 compound names floating off the dry surface like heat shimmer. A clock fast-forwards through months and years; the SVOCs continue drifting. A split screen compares two walls: one with standard "zero-VOC" paint leaking SVOCs, one with a Red List Free mineral paint showing a clean emission profile.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **Low-VOC and zero-VOC paints are still significantly better than pre-regulation conventional paints** (which could exceed 250–380 g/L VOC)
- Mineral/lime paints have limited color range and different application properties
- ECOS Paints has a smaller distribution network and higher cost per gallon
- **71% of US architectural coatings now sold at ≤50 g/L VOC** (up from 18% in 2015) — genuine progress
- The SVOC story is an emerging research area; long-term health effects at indoor concentrations are not yet fully characterized
- EPD coverage for US interior paints is estimated at less than 5% of products — a major data gap

## 3. Flooring: the replacement cycle changes everything

### The data

The NWFA (National Wood Flooring Association) published industry-wide EPDs developed by the Athena Sustainable Materials Institute (2023) comparing flooring types over a **75-year commercial building reference service life, cradle-to-grave.** The results are striking:

| Flooring Type | GWP (kg CO₂e/m², 75-yr cradle-to-grave) | Service Life | Replacements in 75 yr |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid hardwood | **9.2** | 75–100+ years | 0 (refinishable) |
| Engineered wood | **11.4** | 20–50 years | 1–2 |
| Ceramic tile | **19.6** | 50–100+ years | 0–1 |
| Carpet tile (best-in-class) | **26.8** | 10–15 years | 4–6 |
| **Rigid core LVP/SPC** | **64.3** | 10–20 years | **4–5** |
| Broadloom carpet | **75.7** | 5–15 years | 5–10 |

**Rigid core LVP has 7× the lifecycle carbon of solid hardwood** — not because a single LVP plank is catastrophically worse, but because it cannot be refinished and must be fully replaced every 10–15 years, sending non-recyclable PVC to landfill each cycle.

Interface carpet tile (CQuest GB backing with recycled nylon) achieves **2.74 kg CO₂e/m² cradle-to-gate (A1–A3)** — the lowest in the carpet category — versus the EC3 conservative benchmark of **8.69 kg CO₂e/m²** for carpet tile. Interface discontinued carbon offset-based "Carbon Neutral Floors" in 2024, pivoting to direct reduction with a goal of carbon negative by 2040.

On health: a Duke University study (2019) found children in homes with all-vinyl flooring had **15× higher urinary concentrations of benzyl butyl phthalate metabolite** than children with no vinyl flooring. Swedish studies found children with PVC bedroom flooring were **1.5× more likely to have doctor-diagnosed asthma**; mothers with vinyl bedroom flooring during pregnancy saw their children **2× more likely to develop asthma.**

**Sources:** NWFA/DHA EPDs (2023), nwfa.org/environmental-product-declarations/. Interface CQuest GB EPD (2024), interface.com/sustainability/epds.html. Stapleton et al., Duke University, presented at AAAS 2019. Ecology Center vinyl flooring reports, ecocenter.org. EC3 tool data (Feb 2024), buildingtransparency.org.

### Quiz question

**"Over a 75-year building life, which flooring has the highest total carbon footprint?"**
- A) Ceramic tile (all that kiln energy)
- B) Carpet tile (all that replacement)
- C) Solid hardwood (all those trees)
- D) Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) ✅

*Reveal: LVP cannot be refinished, lasts 10–15 years, and goes to landfill each cycle. Over 75 years, you'll install it 4–5 times. Hardwood can be refinished 6–7 times and last a century. The result: LVP's lifecycle carbon is 7× higher than solid hardwood.*

### Named products

- **Lowest carbon (hardwood):** NWFA member products with updated 2025 EPDs — nwfa.org
- **Lowest carbon (carpet tile):** Interface CQuest GB — 2.74 kg CO₂e/m² (A1–A3); ReEntry take-back program; EPD at interface.com
- **PVC-free resilient alternative:** Shaw EcoWorx Resilient — Declare label, HPD, EPD, GREENGUARD Gold; 5.2 kg CO₂e/m²
- **Health concern (LVP):** Conventional LVT/LVP from most manufacturers lacks Declare labels (PVC is Red Listed); phthalates removed from major brands post-2015 but replaced by DOTP at 8–23% by weight
- **Ceramic benchmark:** TCNA 2020 Industry-Wide EPD covering >85% of North American production

### Animation concept

A timeline bar represents 75 years. Hardwood is installed once; a "refinish" icon appears at years 15, 30, 45, 60 — each time the floor resets to new condition. Below it, LVP installs at year 0, then rips out and reinstalls at years 12, 24, 36, 48, 60 — each tearout sends a pile to an animated landfill that grows. Two running carbon counters diverge: hardwood stays at 9.2; LVP climbs through 13, 26, 39, 52, 64.3. The visitor can drag a slider to any year and see both counters.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **LVP has lower upfront cost** ($2–7/ft² installed vs. $8–15+ for hardwood), which drives its explosive market adoption
- **The NWFA comparison was developed by the wood flooring industry** — however, it uses Athena Institute methodology and third-party verified EPD data
- Interface carpet tile numbers are **best-in-class, not industry-average** — the EC3 benchmark (8.69) is more representative
- **Cradle-to-gate comparisons are misleading** without service life context — a critical exhibit teaching point
- Polished concrete GWP varies enormously (**30–60 kg CO₂e/m²** depending on mix design) — no single representative number exists
- LVP health concerns center on **plasticizer migration through dust** (SVOCs), not acute VOC off-gassing — FloorScore certification does not capture this exposure pathway

## 4. Window frames: where 87% of a window's carbon hides

### The data

A peer-reviewed LCA by Salazar et al. (*Journal of Building Engineering*, 2021, DOI: 10.1016/j.jobe.2021.102552) found that in aluminum-framed windows, **the frame accounts for 60–80% of total embodied impacts.** A referenced study by Seo et al. pushed this further: aluminum is the main contributor to **87% of a window's embodied carbon** due to smelting energy.

Frame-only GWP comparison from Salazar (2012, *Buildings*, DOI: 10.3390/buildings2040542):

| Frame Material | GWP per Window (frame only) | Ratio to Wood |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminum | **486 kg CO₂e** | **3.7×** |
| PVC/Vinyl | **258 kg CO₂e** | 2.0× |
| Wood | **130 kg CO₂e** | 1.0× |

Whole-window comparison from Roncone et al. (*Energies*, 2021, DOI: 10.3390/en14133788), reviewing EPDs:

| Frame Material | Double-Glazed GWP (kg CO₂e/m² window) | Triple-Glazed |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminum | **105** | 142 |
| PVC/Vinyl | **68** | 101 |
| Wood | **54–55** | — |

Fiberglass is the emerging contender. Cascadia Windows (British Columbia) published the **first fenestration EPD worldwide under the 2023 PCR**, with frames containing ~58% recycled content and carrying a Living Building Challenge Red List Free designation. UBC research measured Cascadia's fiberglass frame at **84.91 kg CO₂e/m² (frame only)** — higher than wood but with superior thermal break performance and no maintenance.

**Sources:** Salazar et al. (2021), *J. Building Engineering* 43:102552. Salazar (2012), *Buildings* 2:542–553. Roncone et al. (2021), *Energies* 14:3788. Zargar (2022), UBC Sustainability Scholars: sustain.ubc.ca. Cascadia Windows EPD (2024): cascadiawindows.com.

### Quiz question

**"In an aluminum-framed window, what percentage of the window's total embodied carbon comes from the frame alone — not the glass?"**
- A) About 20%
- B) About 40%
- C) 60–87% ✅
- D) The glass is always worse

*Reveal: Aluminum smelting is so energy-intensive that the frame — just the metal surround — can account for up to 87% of a window's embodied carbon. Switching from aluminum to wood frames can cut frame carbon by nearly 4×.*

### Named products

- **Lowest embodied carbon:** Wood-framed windows (Marvin, Andersen A-Series) — Andersen published 14 EPDs across 3 lines (Nov 2025)
- **Best-in-class fiberglass:** Cascadia Windows Universal Series — first EPD under 2023 PCR, Red List Free, ~58% recycled content
- **Highest embodied carbon:** Standard aluminum-framed curtain wall systems
- **PVC concern:** Vinyl frames contain ~57% chlorine by weight; dioxin risk at end-of-life incineration; PVC on ILFI Red List

### Animation concept

An exploded-view window rotates in 3D. Each component — frame, glass pane 1, gas fill, glass pane 2, spacer, hardware — lights up with its carbon share as a proportional bar. The aluminum frame bar dominates at 87%. The visitor taps to swap frame materials: wood shrinks the bar to ~30%; fiberglass lands in between. A "what if" toggle shows: "If every aluminum window in this building were wood-framed, the carbon savings would equal ___."

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **Operational energy dominates lifecycle** — a high-performance triple-glazed aluminum window may save more operational carbon than its embodied premium over 30+ years
- **Recycled aluminum** (secondary) requires ~95% less energy than primary; specifying recycled content dramatically changes the calculation
- Fiberglass windows have **limited manufacturer options** and higher upfront cost
- Wood frames require **maintenance** (painting/staining every 5–10 years) that aluminum and vinyl do not
- **Vinyl/PVC frames are the most affordable option** and dominate the residential market; their moderate GWP is offset by chlorine chemistry and end-of-life concerns
- The 60–80% claim applies specifically to **aluminum frames**; for wood frames, the percentage is below 30%

## 5. Exterior cladding: the material that outlasts the mortgage

### The data

The Brick Industry Association's updated 2025 industry-wide EPD, covering 29 facilities and 39.3% of US production, establishes a **reference service life of 150 years** with **no replacement needed** within a 75-year building lifespan. Cradle-to-gate GWP is approximately **503 kg CO₂e per m³ of brick** — for a standard 90mm veneer, roughly **45 kg CO₂e/m² of wall area** (excluding mortar).

CertainTeed's MainStreet vinyl siding EPD assumes a 50-year service life, but independent sources consistently cite **20–40 years** as practical life before fading, brittleness, and replacement. James Hardie's fiber cement EPD (EPD-IES-0005037:003, International EPD System) documents a **50-year service life** with CO₂ reabsorption (recarbonation) during the use phase — the cementite matrix literally re-absorbs atmospheric carbon over decades. Over 90% of James Hardie products by revenue now have EPDs.

| Cladding | Service Life | Annualized Impact | Key Differentiator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brick | **100–150+ years** | Very low | No replacement; no maintenance |
| Fiber cement (James Hardie) | **50–100 years** | Low | CO₂ reabsorption; US-manufactured |
| Metal panel (aluminum) | **40–60 years** | Moderate | Infinitely recyclable; high initial GWP |
| Wood (cedar) | **20–40 years** (maintained) | Moderate | Biogenic carbon; requires painting/staining |
| Vinyl siding | **20–40 years** | Highest annualized | PVC; poor recyclability; fading |

**Sources:** BIA 2025 Industry-Wide EPD: BusinessWire, Dec 1, 2025. CertainTeed MainStreet Vinyl Siding EPD: certainteed.widen.net. James Hardie EPD (S-P-05037): environdec.com/library/epd5037. Carbon Leadership Forum, "Façade Design Addresses Climate Change": carbonleadershipforum.org.

### Quiz question

**"Vinyl siding has lower upfront embodied carbon than brick. But over 100 years, which actually has the lower total carbon footprint?"**
- A) Vinyl — it's always lighter
- B) They're about equal
- C) Brick — because you never replace it ✅
- D) It depends entirely on the color

*Reveal: Brick's service life of 150+ years means zero replacements over a building's life. Vinyl siding needs replacing every 20–40 years — and each replacement sends non-recyclable PVC to landfill. Annualized over a century, brick's high upfront carbon becomes the lower-impact choice.*

### Named products

- **Longest life, highest transparency:** Brick from BIA member manufacturers (Acme Brick TX, General Shale TN, Glen-Gery PA) — 2025 industry EPD
- **Mid-range with strong EPD:** James Hardie HardiePlank — EPD S-P-05037; US manufacturing (Peru IL + multiple plants); 30-year warranty
- **Vinyl baseline:** CertainTeed MainStreet — EPD available; manufactured Jackson MI, Claremont NC, Hagerstown MD
- **PVC concern:** Vinyl siding is PVC; Red Listed by ILFI; poor practical recyclability despite industry claims of 99% manufacturing scrap recovery

### Animation concept

Five cladding materials are installed side-by-side on an animated building facade. A clock fast-forwards through decades. Vinyl fades and cracks at year 25 — a crew arrives to tear it off and truck it to landfill, then installs fresh vinyl. Wood darkens and peels without maintenance. Fiber cement weathers gracefully. Brick sits unchanged. At year 100, a scoreboard shows total replacements (vinyl: 3–4; brick: 0) and cumulative landfill waste tonnage.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **Brick has the highest upfront cost and weight**, requiring more robust structural support
- **Vinyl siding is the most affordable** and dominates US residential construction; it's the market default
- Vinyl siding industry EPDs **assume 50-year service life** — this is disputed by independent sources citing 20–40 years
- James Hardie's recarbonation benefit is quantified using the GCCA methodology — a relatively new and still-debated approach
- **LMN Architects** found up to a **58% carbon difference** between precast and thin-brick-on-metal-stud wall systems — assembly design matters as much as material choice

## 6. Roofing membrane: where climate chemistry meets the heat island

### The data

The EPDM Roofing Association's peer-reviewed LCA (2010, ISO 14040/14044, TRACI methodology) compared low-slope roofing membranes on a per-m² basis:

| Membrane | GWP (kg CO₂e/m²) | Initial SRI | Service Life |
|---|---|---|---|
| White EPDM (fully adhered) | **~22** | 80+ | 30–50+ years |
| White TPO 60-mil | **~30–31** | **82–97** | 20–30 years |
| White PVC (adhered) | **~73** | 80+ | 25–30 years |
| SBS Modified Bitumen | **~82** | <20 | 20–30 years |

**PVC roofing membrane has over 2× the GWP of EPDM or TPO** — plus chlorine chemistry concerns. Flexible PVC requires plasticizers at **40–60% of product weight**, primarily phthalates (DINP) that are not chemically bound to the polymer and can migrate and leach. PVC production involves vinyl chloride monomer, classified as a **known human carcinogen (IARC Group 1)**. End-of-life incineration of PVC generates dioxins/furans. PVC is on the ILFI Red List.

The albedo story adds complexity. Black EPDM — the lowest-carbon membrane — has an SRI of **<10**, making it a significant heat island contributor. White TPO offers the best combination: low GWP (~30) with the **highest SRI (82–97 initial, 64–90 aged)**, meeting ENERGY STAR, LEED v4, and California Title 24 requirements.

**Sources:** ERA/GreenTeam LCA (2010): epdmroofs.org. Carlisle TPO EPD (ASTM-EPD425, 2023): pcr-epd.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com. DOE Cool Roofs Guide (2010): eere.energy.gov. CRRC certified values. Thornton, "Environmental Impacts of PVC Building Materials": habitablefuture.org. Health Care Without Harm PVC briefing (2021). SPRI Industry-Average TPO EPD: transparencycatalog.com.

### Quiz question

**"Among white single-ply roofing membranes, one type has over twice the embodied carbon of the others — and contains a known carcinogen in its production chain. Which is it?"**
- A) TPO (thermoplastic olefin)
- B) EPDM (synthetic rubber)
- C) PVC (polyvinyl chloride) ✅
- D) They're all about the same

*Reveal: PVC roofing membrane carries ~73 kg CO₂e/m² — more than double the ~22–31 for EPDM or TPO. Its production involves vinyl chloride monomer (IARC Group 1 carcinogen), it requires phthalate plasticizers at 40–60% by weight, and incineration at end of life can produce dioxins.*

### Named products

- **Lowest GWP:** Elevate (formerly Firestone) RubberGard EPDM — EPD available; up to 40-year service life
- **Best GWP + SRI combo:** Carlisle TPO — EPD ASTM-EPD425 (2023); IKO InnoviTPO SRI 97 initial
- **Health/chemistry concern:** Sika Sarnafil PVC — long track record (since 1962) but chlorine chemistry; ~73 kg CO₂e/m²
- **EPD landscape:** 24 current EPDs from 9 manufacturers across all membrane types (epd.guide, 2026)

### Animation concept

Three roof membranes are laid side-by-side. Sunlight hits all three. An infrared camera view shows the black EPDM absorbing heat (surface temp rises to 170°F); white TPO and PVC reflect it (staying near ambient). Then a "flip" reveals the underside — the carbon story. PVC's molecular chain shows chlorine atoms highlighted in red; an end-of-life scenario shows dioxin molecules forming during combustion. A dashboard shows GWP, SRI, service life, and health rating for each membrane, updating as the visitor selects options.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- **The ERA-funded LCA may favor EPDM** — it was commissioned by the EPDM trade group, though it was peer-reviewed per ISO 14040/14044
- **PVC has excellent chemical resistance and heat-welded seams** that make it preferred for certain applications (chemical exposure, ponding water)
- **TPO's track record is shorter** (since 1990s) than EPDM (since 1960s) — earlier TPO formulations had UV degradation issues
- EPDM's longer service life (40–50 yr vs. TPO's 20–30 yr) means its **annualized carbon is even lower**
- **Vegetated (green) roofs** extend membrane life, manage stormwater, and reduce heat island but add structural load and upfront embodied carbon
- The PVC industry argues modern production controls dioxin contribution to <0.5% of total and that high-MW phthalates have low bioavailability

## 7. Acoustical ceiling tiles: the 29% gap nobody discusses

### The data

Armstrong World Industries publishes product-specific EPDs for its two dominant ceiling tile platforms, enabling a direct apples-to-apples comparison:

| Product | Type | GWP A1–A3 (kg CO₂e/ft²) | GWP A1–A3 (kg CO₂e/m²) | Recycled Content | NRC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Armstrong Ultima | Mineral fiber | **0.423** | 4.55 | Up to 95% | 0.75 |
| Armstrong Optima | Fiberglass | **0.546** | 5.87 | Lower | 0.95 |

**Fiberglass ceiling tile has 29% higher cradle-to-gate GWP than mineral fiber** despite being lighter — due to energy-intensive glass fiber production. Armstrong's mineral fiber tiles contain **65–70% mineral wool, 15–20% perlite, and 5–10% corn starch** with recycled content ranging from **24% to 95%** depending on the product line.

Armstrong's Ceiling Recycling Program (operating since 1999) has reclaimed **over 200 million ft²** of old ceiling tiles. Recycling 1 ton of tiles saves **11 tons of raw materials and 1,892 gallons of potable water.** CertainTeed's Symphony m mineral fiber tile is designated **Red List Free** by ILFI with up to 62% recycled content.

**Sources:** Armstrong Ultima EPD #591 (Feb 2024): armstrongceilings.com/content/dam/armstrongceilings/commercial/north-america/epds/ultima-epd.pdf. Armstrong Optima EPD #870 (Jan 2025). CertainTeed product documentation: certainteed.com. Armstrong recycling program FAQ.

### Quiz question

## 8. Gypsum board: small percentages, national-scale impact

### The data

The US consumes approximately **28 billion ft² of gypsum wallboard annually** (~2.6 billion m²). The Gypsum Association's industry-average LCA (Athena Institute, 2011, 17 plants) provides the baseline:

| Product | GWP A1–A3 (per 1,000 ft²) | GWP (per m²) |
|---|---|---|
| ½" Regular | **233 kg CO₂e** | 2.51 |
| 5/8" Type X | **315 kg CO₂e** | 3.39 |
| USG EcoSmart 5/8" (lightweight) | **~249 kg CO₂e** (21% less) | ~2.68 |

USG's Sheetrock EcoSmart Panels achieve **up to 21% lower GWP** through **22% weight reduction** (1.8 vs. ~2.2 lb/ft²), requiring less drying energy — the single largest GWP contributor in wallboard manufacturing (68–81% of total). At national scale, if all 5/8" Type X board were replaced with lightweight equivalents, the annual savings would approach **1.1 million metric tons CO₂e.**

The FGD gypsum story adds urgency. Currently **~34% of US gypsum supply** comes from flue gas desulfurization at coal power plants — a higher-purity byproduct than mined gypsum. As coal plants close, this supply is declining, forcing a return to more mining with greater land disturbance, energy use, and transportation emissions. This transition is underway now and accelerating.

On waste: approximately **330,700 tons per year of gypsum board waste** comes from new construction alone (over-ordering, off-cuts). Most goes to landfill, where gypsum under wet anaerobic conditions produces **hydrogen sulfide gas** — toxic at high concentrations and malodorous at trace levels.

**Sources:** Bushi and Meil, "A Cradle-to-Gate LCA of Gypsum Wallboard," Athena Institute for Gypsum Association, 2011: gypsum.org. USG EcoSmart EPD #141167. USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2025: pubs.usgs.gov. Architecture 2030, Carbon Smart Materials Palette: materialspalette.org/gypsum-board/.

## 9. Adhesives and sealants: the chemicals nobody asked about

### The data

This is the product category with the **largest transparency gap** in commercial construction. The vast majority of adhesives, sealants, and air barrier products have **no HPD, no Declare label, and no EPD.** Major brands including 3M, Sika, DAP, Loctite/Henkel, GE Silicone, and OSI have minimal or no ingredient disclosure through these frameworks. This absence is itself the exhibit's central data point for this category.

VOC content ranges are enormous. SCAQMD Rule 1168 limits reveal the spectrum: indoor carpet adhesive is capped at **50 g/L**, but PVC welding adhesive is permitted at **510 g/L** and architectural sealant primers for porous substrates at **775 g/L.** Solvent-based construction adhesives run **250–510 g/L VOC**, while hybrid polymer alternatives achieve as low as **8 g/L.**

**PROSOCO R-Guard** stands as the transparency benchmark. Five R-Guard air barrier products carry **Declare labels with Red List Free designation**, ingredients disclosed to 0.01% (100 ppm), and HPDs completed. Notably, PROSOCO originally formulated R-Guard with phthalate plasticizers but **reformulated to remove them** during the Bullitt Center Living Building Challenge project (2011), replacing phthalates with polypropylene glycol.

On PFAS: the Green Science Policy Institute (2023) documents PFAS use in roofing membranes (PVDF/FEVE coatings), air/vapor barriers, caulks, adhesives, paints, and floor finishes — used for weatherproofing, corrosion prevention, and chemical resistance. Benjamin Moore's Ultra Spec is the **only BM paint line confirmed PFAS-free** via Declare label.

All polyurethane sealants and adhesives contain **isocyanates (MDI, TDI, HDI)** — known respiratory sensitizers on the ILFI Red List. Hybrid STP (silyl-terminated polyether/polyurethane) sealants are emerging as isocyanate-free, solvent-free, very low-VOC alternatives.

**Sources:** SCAQMD Rule 1168 (amended Oct 2017). PROSOCO R-Guard Declare labels: living-future.org/declare. Green Science Policy Institute, "PFAS in Building Materials" (2023): greensciencepolicy.org. BuildingGreen, "Keeping PFAS Forever Chemicals out of Building Products" (Sept 2023). ILFI Red List: living-future.org/red-list/.

### Tradeoffs and caveats

- No authoritative study quantifies total kg of adhesives/sealants per m² of building — a documented research gap
- **PFAS provide genuine performance benefits** (weatherproofing, chemical resistance) — PFAS-free alternatives may have durability tradeoffs in demanding exposures

## 10. Countertops: the surface that's killing its fabricators

### The data

Engineered quartz countertops contain **90–93% crystalline silica** bound with ~7–10% polyester resin. During fabrication (cutting, grinding, polishing), this generates respirable crystalline silica dust at levels that have caused an **epidemic of accelerated silicosis** — a rapid-onset, irreversible, often fatal lung disease.

In California alone, **over 430 workers have been confirmed with silicosis from engineered stone, 25 have died, and 48 have undergone lung transplants** (2019–2025). A JAMA Internal Medicine study (2023) of 52 California patients found 20 with progressive massive fibrosis and 11 referred for transplant. Cal/OSHA inspections found **25% of fabricator breathing-zone samples exceeded the OSHA PEL of 50 μg/m³**, and **51% of inspected shops had at least one employee above the limit.** Nearly all affected workers are Latino men, some in their 20s. An estimated **100,000 stone fabricators** in the US are at potential risk.

Australia **banned engineered stone effective July 1, 2024** — the world's first such ban — after finding **22.4% of stonemasons diagnosed with silicosis.** In the US, OSHA launched a focused inspection initiative targeting engineered stone in September 2023, and a Chicago fabricator was fined over $1 million in August 2024 after employees were exposed to silica levels **6× the PEL.**

On embodied carbon, the Natural Stone Institute's industry-wide EPD shows natural stone countertops at **46.8 kg CO₂e/m² (A1–A3)** versus engineered quartz at **102.6 kg CO₂e/m²** — an average across Corian Quartz, Vicostone, and Lotte EPDs. **Natural stone has 54% lower embodied carbon than engineered quartz.**

| Material | GWP A1–A3 (kg CO₂e/m²) | Crystalline Silica | Fabrication Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Engineered quartz | **102.6** | 90–93% | **Extreme** (silicosis epidemic) |
| Natural stone | **46.8** | 10–50% (granite); <10% (marble) | Moderate (dust controls needed) |
| Solid surface (Corian) | No EPD found | **0%** | Low |
| Butcher block | No EPD found | 0% | Negligible |

**Sources:** OSHA Focused Inspection Initiative (Sept 2023): osha.gov. Rose et al., MMWR 2019;68:813–818. JAMA Internal Medicine (2023). Lancet Respiratory Medicine editorial, Jan 2024, DOI: 10.1016/S2213-2600(24)00007-9. Natural Stone Institute Embodied Carbon: naturalstoneinstitute.org/programs/sustainability/embodied-carbon-and-natural-stone/. Safe Work Australia engineered stone ban: safeworkaustralia.gov.au/esban.

## The exhibit's deeper argument: we decide without data

Across all 10 categories, a pattern emerges that is more powerful than any single comparison. The materials with the worst environmental and health profiles are often the ones with the **least transparent data.** Conventional adhesives, vinyl flooring, spray foam, and commodity paints dominate their markets while disclosing almost nothing through standardized frameworks. The products that publish EPDs, HPDs, and Declare labels — Interface carpet tile, PROSOCO air barriers, ECOS Paints, ROCKWOOL insulation, Armstrong ceiling tiles — tend to be the better-performing options. Transparency and performance correlate because manufacturers who invest in disclosure also invest in reformulation.

Three statistics frame this gap in ways the "40% of global emissions" figure no longer can:

- **The blowing agent in a single layer of legacy XPS insulation can carry more global warming potential than all other building materials in the wall assembly combined** (KPMB Lab, 2021)
- **Over 430 workers in one US state have contracted an incurable lung disease from a countertop material that 93% of specifiers choose without knowing its silica content** (Cal/OSHA, 2019–2025)
- **A "zero-VOC" paint label is tested using a method the EPA itself says "may not properly assess all of the VOCs emitted from the product"** (EPA Technical Overview)

The exhibit's ultimate message is not that architects should memorize GWP numbers. It's that **tools for transparent material comparison exist** — EC3, HPD Public Repository, Declare database, Pharos — and that specifying without consulting them is the architectural equivalent of prescribing medicine without reading the label.
